# Job-fit rubric and local-model evaluation — 2026-10-29

Previously the model directly supplied the percentage; in one fictional case the score changed from 85 to 50 after a prompt change. The new `criteria-v1` path asks the model to interpret distinct requirements and preferences, cite an exact excerpt from the relevant source, classify each as supported, contradicted or unknown, and cite a candidate fact for supported or contradicted criteria. The application validates the structure, source excerpts and fact indices and computes the score itself. Invalid interpretations are rejected after at most one repair.

For the validated criteria, required items have weight 3 and preferred items weight 1. The lower bound counts only supported weight; the upper bound also counts unknown weight. The displayed midpoint assigns half weight to unknown criteria. Contradicted criteria contribute zero. Values are rounded to the nearest whole percentage. Thus, for identical interpreted criteria, the calculation is deterministic and independent of their ordering. Unknown is neither asserted to pass nor fail; the range is displayed. This is an explicit heuristic, not a probability, a calibrated predictor of hiring, or a guarantee of overall fit. Older assessments become stale when the scoring version changes.

The [first live rubric run](production-fit-rubric-before-2026-10-29.txt) failed because the local model fabricated exact excerpts and added an empty preference criterion. After refining the prompt and safely ignoring only a specific empty-preference placeholder, the [final run](production-fit-rubric-final-2026-10-29.txt) passed after one repair. A [repeat](production-fit-rubric-repeat-2026-10-29.txt) returned the same 55% midpoint and 10–100% range for the fictional job, again after one repair. The model marked Python proficiency unknown despite identifying relevant Python data-cleaning experience; the other two required qualifications were also unknown. Two repetitions of one synthetic case do not establish broad repeatability or accuracy.

The scoring arithmetic is fixed, but the model still decides which criteria matter, their importance and their relationship to facts. Exact quotation and fact references do not prove semantic entailment, distinctness or complete coverage. Candidate facts are currently preselected using word overlap, and the model sees at most 20 selected facts, 2,000 characters of preferences, and 8,000 characters of listing; relevant evidence or requirements may be omitted. The range covers unknown statuses among extracted criteria, not omitted criteria or model mistakes. Broader, independently labelled evaluations and more capable semantic retrieval remain necessary before claiming the score represents overall fit well.

The live tests used local `qwen3.5:9b` Q4_K_M, digest `c97eb11d70b1acdc88af01eef566c1fe4f7fbe93eb1afc06871132f293ff425a`, with one fully GPU-resident model and a 4,096-token context. The repeat preflight recorded 24.28 GiB free system memory and 10.95 GiB dedicated GPU use; a later sample was 24.62 GiB and 11.00 GiB. Samples are not continuous peaks. The ignored test requires external preflight, explicitly requests the preloaded context and uses a no-default-features build. No real candidate data, employer website or application submission was involved.

## Repeatability check after scoring diagnostics

On 2026-10-29, the production assessment path was run three times sequentially on a single fictional candidate and job using the same local `qwen3.5:9b` digest and a fully GPU-resident 4,096-token context. The [retained test output](production-fit-repeatability-asserted-2026-10-29.txt) reports three valid scores of 87%, with the same extracted criteria, statuses and cited evidence in all runs. The ignored live test now asserts equality of both scores and the order-independent rubric fields; it does not require identical explanatory prose. The test took 58.68 seconds after compilation. Resource samples during the run showed approximately 22 GiB free system RAM and 10.7 GiB dedicated GPU use; they are not continuous peak measurements.

The fixture returned Python and accessibility as supported and SQL and cloud deployment as unknown. The model also counted both remote eligibility and the remote preference; the app flagged possible overlap, but that warning does not remove duplicate weight. It treated a requirement for accessibility experience as evidence that the role meets a preference for accessible product development, which may overstate what the listing establishes. Three repetitions of one fictional case, even with identical rubrics, do not establish accuracy, calibration, or repeatability on other jobs, models, or hardware. Further independently labelled and varied cases remain necessary.
