# Bounded semantic fact selection — 2026-10-29

Job-fit assessment no longer relies solely on the 20 facts with the most shared words. From the first 500 reviewed facts, it builds a pool of up to 60: the existing lexical candidates plus an evenly spaced sample of the inventory. If the pool contains more than 20 facts, the local model selects relevant facts in sequential batches, including relationships expressed in different words. At most six batches are sent, each with at most ten facts and 3,000 bytes of fact text; selection uses bounded prefixes of the job and preferences. At most 20 facts enter the assessment. If selection fails or returns nothing, lexical candidates are used as a reported fallback.

The assessment records bounded inventory and pool sizes, submitted and successfully evaluated counts, model-selected and fallback counts, failed batches, selection and batch limits, and whether selection saw shortened job or preference text. The UI exposes these limits. The assessment digest binds the complete bounded 500-fact inventory and the pool, so an edit outside the pool invalidates a saved result. This is a bounded hybrid retrieval stage, not exhaustive semantic search: sampling, batch limits, prefixes, the 20-fact cap, and model errors can omit relevant qualifications.

The [first complete fictional run](semantic-fit-before-2026-10-29.txt) selected an accessibility-related paraphrase without matching the job's wording, but the following rubric assessment failed because the model returned overlapping criteria twice. It also incorporated unrelated fallback facts into its summary. After reducing fallback to failures or empty selection and clarifying the atomic-excerpt instruction, the [repeat](semantic-fit-after-2026-10-29.txt) selected the relevant fact and produced an assessment. A separate [missing-qualifications check](semantic-fit-unknown-2026-10-29.txt) still returned a 55% midpoint with a 10–100% range. These are two fictional cases, not evidence of broad semantic recall or calibrated match quality. Model-selected facts and criterion interpretations can still be wrong; score ranges do not account for omitted criteria or facts.

The live tests used local `qwen3.5:9b` Q4_K_M, digest `c97eb11d70b1acdc88af01eef566c1fe4f7fbe93eb1afc06871132f293ff425a`, with one fully GPU-resident model and a 4,096-token context. Before the successful complete run, sampled free system memory was 23.69 GiB and dedicated GPU usage 11.08 GiB; a later sample was 24.02 GiB and 11.02 GiB. These are point-in-time samples, not continuous peaks. The ignored test requires external resource preflight and uses a no-default-features build. No real candidate data, employer website, or application submission was used.
